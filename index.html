Research

Machine Learning-Guided Raman Spectral Decomposition

Senior thesis research for Raman spectroscopy metrology, focused on decomposing complex measured spectra into baseline and overlapping peak components so calibration workflows can become more interpretable, automatable, and robust.

2.9M parameters baseline + up to 3 peaks 512-point windows 602 spectra 19,753 pure-peak matches 0.9999 mean R2 Calibration R2 up to 0.982

Problem

Raman spectra from industrial gas analyzers contain baselines, overlapping peaks, and concentration-dependent behavior that make direct peak isolation difficult to trust in calibration and diagnostics.

Approach

Built a machine-learning-guided decomposition pipeline that predicts a baseline plus up to 3 overlapping peak components across 512-point spectral windows, then validates extracted peaks against center-primary matching logic.

Architecture

Implemented a 2.9M-parameter PyTorch 1D U-Net for spectral-window decomposition, followed by physics-aware fitting, matching, residual analysis, and calibration evaluation.

Results

Evaluated 602 spectra, produced 19,753 pure-peak matches, achieved 0.9999 mean R2 reconstruction accuracy, and reached calibration R2 up to 0.982 for ethane/propane.

Tech Stack

Python, PyTorch, NumPy, pandas, spectroscopy data pipelines, Matplotlib, calibration modeling, peak matching, and residual diagnostics.

What I Learned

Useful ML in measurement systems is not just a model score. It needs validation hooks, interpretable failure modes, calibration context, and enough tooling discipline for engineers to challenge the result.
